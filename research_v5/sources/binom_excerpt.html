Gridtown USA, besides having excellent donut shops, is known for its precisely laid out grid of streets and avenues. Streets run east-west, and avenues north-south, for the entire stretch of the town, never curving and never interrupted by parks or schools or the like.
Suppose you live on the corner of 8th and 8th and work on the corner of 17th and 17th. Thus you must travel 18 blocks to get to work as quickly as possible.
How many different routes can you take to work, assuming you want to get there as quickly as possible?
Now suppose you want to stop and get a donut on the way to work, from your favorite donut shop on the corner of 14th Ave. and 13th St. How many routes to work, stopping at the donut shop, can you take (again, ensuring the shortest possible route)?
Disaster Strikes Gridtown: there is a pothole on 9th Ave. between 11th St. and 12th St. How many routes to work can you take avoiding that unsightly (and dangerous) stretch of road?
The pothole has been repaired (phew!) and a new donut shop has opened on the corner of 9th Ave. and 11th St. How many routes to work drive by one or the other (or both) donut shops? Hint: the donut shops serve PIE.
Suppose you are ordering a calzone from D.P. Dough. You want 5 distinct toppings, chosen from their list of 8 vegetarian toppings.
How many choices do you have for your calzone?
How many choices do you have for your calzone if you refuse to have green pepper as one of your toppings?
How many choices do you have for your calzone if you insist on having green pepper as one of your toppings?
How do the three questions above relate to each other? Do you see why this makes sense?
