## Metrics, uncertainty and baseline meaning

FCR = erroneous specifications ACCEPTed / known semantic-error opportunities. Acceptance coverage = ACCEPT / all rows. Decision coverage = (ACCEPT + REJECT) / all rows. Selective risk = erroneous ACCEPTs / semantically resolved ACCEPTs. Invalid outputs and unknown relations are reported explicitly; unknowns do not become correct cases. A zero error denominator means N/A, not zero.

For the injected challenge: FCR 0/72; nominal Wilson 95% interval 0–5.07%; selective risk 0/8 with upper bound 32.44%; acceptance coverage 8/96 = 8.33%; decision coverage 80/96 = 83.33%. Eight correct unsupported cases and eight ambiguous cases account for the 16 abstentions. False rejection is 0/16 correct cases. Accept-all, Solver-only and cheap-rule each accept all 72 errors on these constructed rows. Exact McNemar arithmetic versus Accept-all gives p=2^-71; dependence prevents a natural-population significance claim.

For the four model errors: FCR 0/4, nominal Wilson 95% interval 0–48.99%; paired exact McNemar versus Accept-all p=0.125. Accept-all and Solver-only falsely certify 4/4; AXIOM-X and cheap-rule certify 0/4. On that model, cheap-rule accepts 40/48 versus AXIOM-X 35/48, so broader abstention lowers AXIOM-X coverage without a demonstrated extra detection in this sample. AXIOM-X abstains on four known correct outputs and falsely rejects none of the 39 correct outputs. Its selective risk is 0/35, nominal upper bound 9.89%. On the stronger model, cheap-rule falsely rejects two correct outputs; this illustrates its syntactic limitations without establishing general architectural superiority.

The two models share the same 48 source tasks; families repeat. Wilson intervals are nominal row-level summaries and the p-values are conditional descriptive calculations. They are not calibrated uncertainty for unrestricted natural-language performance. No zero count establishes zero population risk.
