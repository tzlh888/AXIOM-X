# Admissions Interview Preparation

**DRAFT FOR REVIEW · reference knowledge for applicant study**

These questions explain the actual project. Reading the answers does not establish unaided technical mastery. In an interview, distinguish what you understand and personally did from engineering performed with substantial AI assistance.

## 1. Why compare solution sets rather than just their sizes?

**Question:** Two specifications return the same count. Are they equivalent?

**Reference answer:** Not necessarily. Let x,y range over {0,1}. The specification x<y admits only (0,1); y<x admits only (1,0). Both counts are one, but the accepted assignments differ. A point in the symmetric difference S△T is a concrete counterexample to equivalence. Exact equivalence requires S=T after a justified variable mapping.

The same meaning can also have different representations. Domain x∈{1,2} with no further condition is equivalent to x∈{0,1,2} with x≠0. The earlier evaluator's domain-tuple shortcut could mislabel this kind of equivalent restriction; comparing full finite semantics corrects that error. Renaming variables without a proved map remains inconclusive.

**Knowledge to study:** finite sets, Cartesian products, cardinality versus equality, bijections, symmetric difference, and semantic versus syntactic equivalence. Reference implementation: `axiom_x/solver.py`; V3 correction history: `research_v4/V3_RELIABILITY_AUDIT.json`.

**Try without assistance:** enumerate both two-variable sets, then explain why a witness refutes equivalence but equal counts cannot prove it.

## 2. What does Z3 prove here, and where does the proof stop?

**Question:** If Z3 finds a satisfying assignment, has AXIOM-X verified the counting answer and natural-language meaning?

**Reference answer:** A single satisfying assignment proves existence for the encoded formula. Counting requires complete finite enumeration. The Z3 path records a model, blocks that full assignment and repeats until UNSAT; Python independently enumerates the finite Cartesian product. Complete matching sets support the computed count for the formal specification. A timeout or resource limit does not establish completeness and should lead to an inconclusive result.

For domain sizes d1,…,dn, exhaustive inspection can require their product assignments; constraints and model storage add cost. The implementation limits variables and search space. Z3's SMT arithmetic is useful, but this prototype is not an unrestricted theorem prover or a general efficient solution to model counting.

The remaining language step is a separate assumption: whether the parser/specification preserves intended meaning. Two solvers can agree on the same mistaken formalization. V4's eligibility checks used the tested parser, so they cannot count as independent human validation.

**Knowledge to study:** SAT versus SMT, satisfiability versus model counting, conjunction, model-blocking clauses, UNSAT, completeness, resource limits and shared modelling errors. Reference: `axiom_x/solver.py`, `axiom_x/audit.py`, and `research_v4/v4_experiment.py::_validate_case`.

**Try without assistance:** count all assignments to three binary variables, impose “exactly two equal 1,” and explain which full assignment a blocking clause removes.

## 3. Does 4/4 detection prove reliability or superiority to a simple rule?

**Question:** How should a mathematician interpret the project's two headline fractions?

**Reference answer:** 72/72 refers to predefined injected semantic errors in a controlled challenge. It demonstrates behaviour on those mechanisms, but repeated templates and parser-conditioned eligibility limit independence. The separate 4/4 refers to errors generated by one local model on controlled inputs. They were fixed-assignment/omitted-constraint failures on modulo-themed tasks; the simple distinctness/nonzero rule also rejected all four.

FCR counts erroneous ACCEPTs divided by error opportunities. Observing 0/4 gives a nominal Wilson 95% upper bound around 49%, and exact paired McNemar versus Accept-all gives p=0.125: four improvements with no reverse discordance yield two-sided p=2(1/2)^4. There are no such improvements over cheap-rule in these four cases. Shared sources further limit population interpretation. When the other model makes zero semantic errors, FCR is undefined.

Coverage must also be reported: AXIOM-X accepted 35/48 of that model's outputs, compared with cheap-rule's 40/48. An abstaining system can avoid errors while providing fewer answers. Natural-language generalization needs independently validated intended semantics, which this study does not yet have.

**Knowledge to study:** conditional rates, zero denominators, selective prediction, confidence intervals, paired tests, dependence and sampling frames. Reference: `research_v4/analysis/model_results.json`, `axiom_x/evaluation_v2.py` and the V5 audit.

**Try without assistance:** explain why “no false certifications were observed” is supported, while “the true false-certification rate is zero” is not. Then state one honest reason the cheap rule can reject a wrong fixed assignment even when that assignment already implies distinctness.
